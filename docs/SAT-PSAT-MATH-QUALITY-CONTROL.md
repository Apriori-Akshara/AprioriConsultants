# Apriori SAT / PSAT — Math Question Quality Control Standard

**Date:** September 12, 2026  
**Status:** Permanent SAT/PSAT Math content source of truth  
**Applies to:** PSAT/NMSQT and Digital SAT Math mocks, drills, quizzes and future Math banks

This document is subordinate only to the master test-prep content standard and the current official SAT Suite specifications. It governs Math authoring, assembly, difficulty, visual/data use, calculator metadata, and release QC.

## 1. Source hierarchy

### Tier 1 — official assessment specification

College Board is the primary authority for:

- assessment structure;
- content domains;
- skill/knowledge testing points;
- module/adaptive design;
- timing;
- calculator environment;
- question formats;
- use of informational graphics;
- PSAT/NMSQT versus SAT domain differences.

Primary references:

- College Board, **Math Specifications – SAT Suite**
- College Board, **Assessment Framework for the Digital SAT Suite**, version 3.01
- College Board, current SAT Math overview
- College Board, current PSAT/NMSQT Math / Student Guide

### Tier 2 — established test-prep benchmarks

Kaplan, Magoosh and PrepScholar may be used to benchmark:

- variety of mathematical approaches;
- quality of distractors;
- hard-question construction;
- data/graph interpretation;
- explanation quality;
- realistic pacing and strategic demand.

These sources are benchmarks, not authority for the official domain percentages. No Apriori question may be copied.

### Tier 3 — Apriori internal rules

Where College Board intentionally does not publish a fixed numerical rule, this document establishes an internal authoring/QC target. Internal targets must always be labeled as internal and must never be presented as College Board requirements.

## 2. Current Math form structure

### SAT

- 44 Math questions;
- two 22-question modules;
- 20 operational + 2 pretest questions per module in the standard form;
- 70 minutes total;
- 35 minutes per module;
- calculator permitted throughout Math.

### PSAT/NMSQT

- 44 Math questions;
- two 22-question modules;
- 20 operational + 2 pretest questions per module in the standard form;
- 70 minutes total;
- 35 minutes per module;
- calculator permitted throughout Math.

Apriori's protected content pools contain 22 Math items for Module 1 and 22 items for each adaptive Module 2 route pool. This is an authoring pool architecture, not a claim that all 22 items are scored operational questions on an official form.

## 3. Official domain distribution

### SAT

| Domain | Official share | 44-question practical range |
|---|---:|---:|
| Algebra | ≈35% | about 13–15 |
| Advanced Math | ≈35% | about 13–15 |
| Problem-Solving and Data Analysis | ≈15% | about 5–7 |
| Geometry and Trigonometry | ≈15% | about 5–7 |

### PSAT/NMSQT

| Domain | Official share | 44-question practical range |
|---|---:|---:|
| Algebra | ≈35% | about 13–15 |
| Advanced Math | ≈32.5% | about 12–14/15 |
| Problem-Solving and Data Analysis | ≈20% | about 7–9 |
| Geometry and Trigonometry | ≈12.5% | about 4–6 |

### Assembly rule

The bank must not use an equal 25% allocation simply because there are four domains.

Every official-style Math module must contain all four domains. Domain counts may vary by module within a valid overall form allocation; the student route must still produce an assessment that stays within the official blueprint when assembled.

## 4. Subtopic coverage

### Algebra

Required coverage across the bank:

- linear equations in one variable;
- linear equations in two variables;
- linear functions;
- systems of two linear equations in two variables;
- linear inequalities in one or two variables.

### Advanced Math

Required coverage across the bank:

- equivalent expressions;
- nonlinear equations in one variable;
- systems of equations in two variables where nonlinear relationships are involved;
- nonlinear functions;
- quadratic, exponential, polynomial, rational, radical and absolute-value relationships where appropriate to the product and grade level.

### Problem-Solving and Data Analysis

Required coverage across the bank:

- ratios, rates, proportional relationships and units;
- percentages;
- one-variable distributions and measures of center/spread;
- two-variable data, models and scatterplots;
- probability and conditional probability;
- inference from sample statistics;
- SAT-only extension: margin of error and evaluating statistical claims through observational studies and experiments.

### Geometry and Trigonometry

SAT coverage:

- area and volume;
- lines, angles and triangles;
- right triangles and trigonometry;
- circles.

PSAT/NMSQT coverage:

- area and volume;
- lines, angles and triangles;
- right triangles and right-triangle trigonometry.

No domain may be represented only by one repeated skill family.

## 5. Figure and data-display standard

College Board does not publish a fixed percentage of Math questions that must contain a graphic. The official framework describes a wide range of informational graphics, including data displays, geometric figures and xy-plane graphs, and College Board student materials describe select Math questions as being accompanied by informational graphics.

The approximately 30% figure published for Math refers to questions **set in context**. It must not be relabeled as “30% graphics.”

### Apriori internal rule

Graphics are governed by purpose, coverage and balance, not by an invented official percentage.

A graphic qualifies as a valid visual item only when the student must use or reason from the visual to answer the question. Decorative duplication of information already fully stated in the prompt does not qualify.

Across the complete bank, deliberately cover:

- tables;
- scatterplots;
- line/xy-plane graphs;
- quadratic/nonlinear graphs;
- other data displays;
- geometry figures;
- coordinate geometry visuals;
- original mixed data/visual stimuli where appropriate.

### Domain-to-visual expectation

- PSDA: frequent data displays, scatterplots, tables and statistical representations.
- Algebra: line/coordinate visuals when the graph is part of the reasoning.
- Advanced Math: nonlinear graphs or representation changes when relevant.
- Geometry: purposeful geometry diagrams rather than automatic decorative figures.

All graphics must be original, mathematically correct, legible and accessible.

## 6. Question-format standard

The Math bank should maintain a working mix near the Digital SAT structure of approximately:

- 75% multiple choice;
- 25% student-produced response (SPR).

The exact displayed form may vary because the official operational content includes pretest items and because the protected bank is larger than one scored route.

SPR items must:

- have a single defensible numeric result;
- state the required entry format clearly;
- contain no visible answer choices;
- avoid relying on ambiguous rounding unless explicitly controlled.

## 7. Difficulty standard

College Board does not publish a single fixed percentage of Easy/Medium/Hard Math questions for every form. It does specify the adaptive principle:

- Module 1 contains a broad mix of easy, medium and hard questions;
- Module 2 contains a mixed-difficulty set targeted higher or lower on average based on Module 1 performance;
- questions within a module are arranged from easier to harder.

### Apriori internal difficulty targets

For each 22-question protected Math pool:

#### Module 1

- Easy: 4–8
- Medium: 7–10
- Hard: 4–8
- all three levels must be represented.

#### High Module 2 route

- Easy: 0–4
- Medium: 6–10
- Hard: 9–14
- average difficulty must be above the Standard route.

#### Standard Module 2 route

- Easy: 3–7
- Medium: 7–11
- Hard: 5–9
- average difficulty must fall between High and Low.

#### Low Module 2 route

- Easy: 6–10
- Medium: 8–12
- Hard: 2–6
- average difficulty must be below Standard.

Across the full 88-question protected Math bank, hard items must be at least 30% of the bank as an internal floor. The purpose is not to claim that College Board uses 30%; it is to ensure Apriori has enough genuinely difficult material to make the adaptive experience credible.

## 8. What makes a Math question genuinely hard

A question must not be classified Hard merely because it uses larger numbers or a less familiar topic.

A genuine Hard item should normally require at least two meaningful difficulty features, such as:

- multi-step reasoning;
- a representation shift;
- non-routine modeling;
- strategic method selection;
- a parameter or constraint inference;
- interpreting a nontrivial data display;
- distinguishing among close distractors;
- combining concepts;
- translating a complex verbal relationship into mathematics;
- deciding what information is necessary or unnecessary.

Hard items should normally use `analyze` or `synthesize` cognitive demand metadata, not merely `apply`.

### Difficulty must come from reasoning, not clutter

Do not make a question hard by:

- adding irrelevant sentences;
- using awkward wording;
- forcing unnecessary arithmetic;
- using huge numbers without conceptual purpose;
- hiding a straightforward calculation behind decorative context;
- adding a graphic the student does not need.

## 9. High-quality difficult-question patterns

The bank should deliberately build difficult Math items in patterns such as:

### Algebra

- systems with a structural shortcut that must be noticed;
- parameter conditions for one/no/multiple solutions;
- linear models requiring interpretation across representations;
- constraints that change which solution is valid.

### Advanced Math

- function transformations combined with parameter reasoning;
- equivalent expressions where the useful factorization or structure is not immediately obvious;
- nonlinear systems requiring strategic substitution/elimination;
- quadratic/exponential relationships requiring interpretation rather than routine solving;
- multiple possible values narrowed by a condition.

### PSDA

- multi-step inference from a table/scatterplot;
- comparing distributions rather than calculating only one statistic;
- conditional probability embedded in a two-way table;
- interpreting sampling or study design limitations;
- selecting or rejecting a model based on data behavior.

### Geometry/Trigonometry

- multi-condition geometry diagrams;
- similarity/congruence with an intermediate inference;
- coordinate geometry combined with geometric relationships;
- right-triangle/trigonometric reasoning where the direct formula is not the whole task;
- composite figures with a non-obvious decomposition.

---

## 10. Distractor standard

Every multiple-choice Math item must have:

- exactly one defensible correct answer;
- three plausible distractors;
- distractors tied to realistic errors or tempting incorrect reasoning.

Avoid distractors generated only by adding/subtracting an arbitrary constant from the answer.

A strong distractor should represent a specific error pattern, such as:

- sign error;
- reversed ratio;
- using diameter instead of radius;
- forgetting to square a factor;
- solving the wrong representation;
- using the wrong conditional denominator;
- interpreting slope/intercept backwards;
- stopping after an intermediate step.

---

## 11. Calculator and reference-sheet standard

The digital SAT/PSAT Math environment permits calculator use throughout Math. The embedded calculator experience is designed to support scientific/graphing work while excluding CAS functionality.

Apriori metadata must distinguish:

- calculator available;
- calculator optional;
- calculator useful;
- calculator required for the intended path.

A calculator must not be used as a substitute for question design. A hard question should remain conceptually hard even though a calculator is available.

---

## 12. Originality and duplication

No Apriori item may copy or lightly paraphrase College Board, Kaplan, Magoosh, PrepScholar, Barron's, or any other source item.

Math duplication QC must reject:

- exact repeated prompts;
- same mathematical structure with trivial number substitution;
- same diagram with superficial label changes;
- same context + same operation + same answer path across mocks;
- repeated high-difficulty item templates that make tests predictable.

Concept repetition is permitted; question repetition is not.

---

## 13. Mathematical correctness review

Before publication every item must pass:

1. **Answer verification** — recompute independently.
2. **Distractor verification** — verify each distractor is wrong under the stated conditions.
3. **Edge-condition review** — check zero, negative, domain, denominator, inequality and boundary cases where relevant.
4. **Unit review** — ensure units and scale factors are consistent.
5. **Graph/figure review** — ensure every plotted point, label, slope, intercept, angle and dimension agrees with the text.
6. **Explanation review** — explanation reaches the answer without hidden assumptions.
7. **Tool review** — calculator/reference-sheet metadata matches the intended solution.

---

## 14. Adaptive assembly QC

For every mock:

- Module 1 must contain all four domains;
- Module 1 must contain Easy, Medium and Hard items;
- High, Standard and Low Module 2 pools must be measurably different in average difficulty;
- High must contain substantially more Hard content than Low;
- route pools must preserve valid domain coverage;
- route assembly must not accidentally select a domain distribution incompatible with the product blueprint.

The adaptive engine is not allowed to compensate for a bad source bank. Distribution and difficulty must be correct in the question bank before routing.

---

## 15. Release gate

A Math bank is `assembly-ready` only when:

- official domain distribution is satisfied;
- required subtopics are represented;
- all four domains occur in every module pool;
- visual/data coverage is purposeful;
- SPR/MCQ mix is within the intended target;
- difficulty profile passes;
- Hard questions contain meaningful hard features;
- adaptive route averages satisfy High > Standard > Low;
- answer and explanation checks pass;
- graphics pass the figure-quality gate;
- duplicate and near-duplicate checks pass;
- original content checks pass.

No production mock should be considered Math-QC approved merely because it loads and scores correctly.
